How it works

Trips run themselves. Once the adapter is connected, a trip starts when the engine starts. Switch off for a fuel stop and the trip pauses. Start again within 15 minutes and it carries on. After 15 minutes off, it closes. Walk away from the car and the app simply loses the adapter. Keep the app open on screen while driving: Android stops web pages in the background.

RPM. The tag shows the most you should rev right now, which rises as the engine warms: 2000 below 40°C, 2500 below 60°C, 3000 below 75°C, 3500 below 85°C, then 4500. Blue: cold but fine. Green: warm and in the efficient zone, 1500–2600. Amber: close to the limit, or labouring at low revs under load. Solid red: over the limit.

Coolant. Exact temperature. COLD below 60°C, WARMING to 80°C, READY from 80°C, HOT above 105°C. After two steady minutes between 80 and 100°C the tile switches to consumption, with the temperature and a tick in the corner. It switches back the moment the temperature leaves that band.

Turbo. OFF: cruising, idling or coasting. ON: working. FULL: near maximum, about 1.0–1.2 bar on your engine. When cold, the tag shows the boost to stay under, and the tile goes red past it. LOW: full throttle above 2000 rpm but under 0.6 bar for a few seconds, which can mean a boost leak or limp mode.

Let it idle. Appears when you stop after a stretch of real boost while moving, for 30–90 seconds depending on how hard the turbo worked. Revving in neutral doesn’t count.

Fuel estimate calibration

The ECU doesn’t report fuel use, so it’s estimated from load and RPM. After a full tank, compare litres pumped with what the app estimated and set the correction here.

Connection

Classic is the adapter’s Android mode and usually the most reliable. Pair “Android-Vlink” once in your phone’s Bluetooth settings with PIN 1234, then tap Connect.
