How to read this

RPM bar. The white line is how high you should rev right now. It moves with engine temperature: 2000 when cold, 2500 below 60°C, 3000 below 75°C, 3500 below 85°C, then 4500 when fully warm. Past the line the bar turns red and the whole panel glows. Amber means you’re close. Green means you’re in the diesel’s efficient zone, 1500–2600 rpm when warm, which is marked with dashed lines.

Coolant. Exact temperature, which your dashboard needle can’t show. COLD below 60°C, WARMING to 80°C, READY from 80°C, HOT above 105°C. While warming it estimates how many minutes until 80°C.

Turbo. Boost is how much extra air the turbo pushes in, in bar above outside pressure. OFF means cruising or idling, normal. ON means the turbo is working. FULL means it’s giving close to everything, about 1.0–1.2 bar on your engine. When the engine is cold, a white line shows the boost to stay under, because the oil hasn’t reached the turbo bearings properly yet. LOW appears if you floor it above 2000 rpm and boost stays under 0.6 bar for a few seconds. That can point to a boost leak, a sticky turbo or limp mode, so tell Claude if you see it often.

Turbo cool-down. After hard driving, when you stop, a ring counts down a minute of idling so the turbo can cool before you switch off.
